Bayesian updating, one count at a time

Reveal the asbestos-fiber counts in sequence and compare how two gamma priors become Poisson-model posteriors.

Poisson–gamma model

The data and the question

Learning the expected count λ

A study counted asbestos fibers in each of 23 grid squares on a filter. Let λ denote the unknown expected number of fibers per square. Conditional on λ, the counts are modeled as independent Poisson observations with common rate λ. A gamma distribution represents uncertainty about λ before the data are observed; the two priors below express contrasting starting beliefs.

Poisson likelihood + two gamma priors

X1, …, X23 | Λ = λ ∼ Poisson(λ), independently and identically distributed Λ ∼ Gamma(a, b)
Posterior ∝ likelihood × prior Prior A: Gamma(9, 0.6) Prior B: Gamma(4, 2/15) Posterior: Gamma(a + Sk, b + k)

Conditional on λ, the counts are assumed independent with the same rate. Gamma uses the shape–rate parameterization; after k squares, Sk is the sum of the observed counts. Prior A has mean 15 and standard deviation 5, while the broader Prior B has mean 30 and standard deviation 15. Comparing them shows how prior assumptions influence early inference and how shared data pull the posteriors together.
