Power of a two-sample mean test

When two population means differ, how likely is a test to detect the difference?

Two independent samples

Compare measurements from two populations with unknown means μX and μY and the same unknown variance σ². Assume independent observations from normal populations. The true difference is Δ = μX − μY.

Power is the probability of rejecting equal means when a specified nonzero difference is true.

The model and hypotheses

X₁, …, Xn ∼ N(μX, σ²),   Y₁, …, Ym ∼ N(μY, σ²)

Each sample is independent and identically distributed; the two samples are independent.

H₀: μX = μYH₁: μX ≠ μY

Try it: increase |Δ| or either sample size, or decrease σ. For a nonzero difference, each change increases power. Raising α also increases power, but allows more false positives. At Δ = 0, both curves coincide and the rejection probability is α.

How the test and the approximation work

The pooled test estimates the common variance from both samples:

Sp² = [(n − 1)SX² + (m − 1)SY²] / (n + m − 2)

T = (X̄ − Ȳ) / [Sp √(1/n + 1/m)]

Here SX² and SY² are sample variances. Under H₀, T has a t distribution with n + m − 2 degrees of freedom. The two-sided test rejects beyond its lower α/2 and upper 1 − α/2 quantiles.

For the plotted approximation, replace Sp by σ and the t cutoffs by ±c, where c is the 1 − α/2 standard-normal quantile:

s = σ √(1/n + 1/m),   δ = Δ / s

T ≈ N(0, 1) under H₀;   T ≈ N(δ, 1) at difference Δ

Power ≈ Φ(−c − δ) + 1 − Φ(c − δ)

Φ is the standard-normal cumulative distribution function. The two terms are the left and right alternative rejection tails. For Δ ≠ 0, β = 1 − power is the probability of missing the difference.

This is approximate power for the pooled t test, and can be less accurate for small samples. It is exact for the corresponding normal test if σ is known.
